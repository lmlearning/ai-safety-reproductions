This paper addresses an empirical evaluation question adjacent to AI alignment and human judgments of model behavior: whether chatbot responses are perceived as more empathetic than healthcare professionals' text responses. No clinical recommendation is being evaluated here.

## Feasibility and reproduced scope

The paper's central contribution is its statistical synthesis of earlier studies. Original Figure 2 exposes the exact 13 standardized effects, their standard errors, and model subgroups. Its Methods specify RevMan 5.4, and its forest plot specifies the generic inverse-variance random-effects method. The archived [original RevMan 5 algorithms](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/statistical-methods-revman5) establish DerSimonian-Laird pooling and subgroup comparisons. No model inference, model weights, API key, or paid service is needed.

Full-paper reproduction would require independent database screening, study-selection and risk-of-bias judgments, original constituent experiments, and their individual ratings. Those activities were not rerun. This is a **central statistical-result reproduction**, not a full-paper reproduction and not fresh LLM behavior generation. It is more than a replot: the study-level original inputs were independently entered, and all pooling, standard errors, uncertainty intervals, hypothesis tests, between-study heterogeneity, subgroup comparison, and forest weights were recomputed from first principles. This scope is consistent with the programme rule that released-data analysis may count when analysis itself is the target paper's central empirical result.
