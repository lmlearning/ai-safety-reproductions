## Fixed procedure

- Monte Carlo: n in {200,1000}, total calibration m in {200,500}, (q0,q1) in {(0.7,0.7),(0.7,0.9),(0.9,0.7),(0.9,0.9)}, theta=0,0.05,...,1; 10,000 trials per point and seed; alpha=0.05; pilot 10 observations per class. Independent vectorized implementation with fresh seeds 1729,1730,1731. Preserve every trial's sufficient counts. Sampling binomial counts replaces sums of independent Bernoulli trials with their identical distributions; it changes the random-number stream, not the statistical experiment.
- The test truth count is sampled first; judge true-positive and false-positive counts are conditional on it. Equal calibration is independent. Adaptive calibration reuses its pilot success counts and samples the additional allocated observations. Pilot-dependent allocation, rounding to even at ties, smoothing, and clipping follow the original source exactly.
- Cross-check independent formulas against the original scalar functions on fixed cases before trials. Also execute the original scalar Figure 4 Monte Carlo for the main configuration and seed 1234 (210,000 trials), retaining its output arrays. Compare its first-trial point estimates to the original vector illustration.
- Figure 2: q0=.7,q1=.9,n=1e9,m0=m1=500,200-point theta grid. Figure 3: p=.3,.5,.7,.9, n=1e9, total m=100..10000 step 10, q0=.7,q1=.9, no pilot, using specified q beliefs. Compare all 3,964 interval lengths to the original functions. Store complete numeric arrays and plots.

## Numerical success criteria

1. Analytic corrected point estimate recovers each theta with absolute error <=1e-12. Independent deterministic interval lengths and original scalar functions agree <=1e-12.
2. For all 16 configurations and 21 theta points, pooled fresh-seed naive and equal-calibration coverages agree with the published SVG curves within 0.020; mean equal and adaptive interval lengths agree within 0.005. These are fixed absolute tolerances for Monte Carlo variation, with vector-coordinate rounding negligible. No failing point is discarded.
3. Original scalar main-run first-trial naive and adjusted point estimates agree with the published SVG points within 0.00001. This assesses whether released code reconstructs the published illustrative random stream.
4. All results finite, all trial counts correct, zero exclusions, saved raw sufficient counts and immutable source/configuration hashes; scalar/vector fixed-case checks pass. A run passing these criteria is a full empirical reproduction, counted once after review.

## Findings recorded before running

The original Figure 4(c) vector curves cross: adaptive intervals are sometimes longer. Accordingly, numerical reconstruction of the actual published results and verification of prose claims are reported separately. We will report all points where the claim of *consistently* shorter intervals fails, together with adaptive coverage (not shown in the paper). Failure of an overstatement is not concealed by numerical agreement. Figure 4(a) is a single trial, not a mean-unbiasedness demonstration. The source clips point estimates to [0,1], introducing boundary bias; exact unbiasedness of the unbounded known-reliability formula is not asserted for the finite-sample clipped estimator. Algorithm 1's final assignment appears reversed in the paper; the released implementation's m0=m-m1 is used. Its p<1e-6 branch allocates m1=m-pilot; retain rather than silently repair it.

No outcome-dependent tolerance changes, extra seeds chosen to rescue an outcome, or modified methods will be used. Failures or corrections require a new immutable run and a disclosed amendment.
